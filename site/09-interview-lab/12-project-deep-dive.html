The Project Deep-Dive Round: Presenting Your Architecture

17 min listen · How to run the ten-minute project architecture deep dive: a seven-segment ribbon (problem, constraints, architecture, decisions and rejected alternatives, numbers, failures, next steps) drawn above a growing diagram, worked through an agentic document-QA assistant for a regulated financial firm — plus handling interruptions, 'why not X', latency metrics, deployment framework choices, and the role-expectations conversation.

Facts verified: 2026-09-25

One-sentence thesis

The deep-dive round is won by structure, not by knowing your project: a ten-minute, seven-segment walkthrough (problem → constraints → architecture → decisions & rejected alternatives → numbers → failures → next) that builds one diagram as one request travels through it, backed by percentile numbers and honest failures, followed by sharp questions about how the team actually ships.

The mental image: the ribbon and the drawing

A whiteboard. Across the top, a ribbon = your ten minutes in seven segments. Below it, the architecture drawing, which only grows when your finger moves one segment right along the ribbon. Interruption? Answer, then put your finger back on the ribbon.

3. Architecture ~3 min Gateway → orchestrator → retrieval (below) → model serving (right) → citation checker → audit log (bottom), eval loop (top)
4. Decisions ~2 min Crossed-out alternatives in the margins

6. Failures ~1.5 min Red "scar" marks on the boxes that broke

Always percentiles (p95/p99), never averages; always a per-component breakdown (gateway, retrieval, prefill/TTFT, decode, checker).

Failure stories (symptom → root cause → fix → guardrail)

  1. Superseded fee schedule quoted with a valid-looking citation. Root cause: re-indexing appended new chunks, deleted sections left orphaned old chunks. Fix: section-level content hashing, version-stamped chunks, tombstoning of removed sections. Guardrail: nightly index-vs-source reconciliation alerting on orphans. (See episode 8.)
  2. Morning p99 TTFT spikes + stuttering streams. Root cause: users pasting whole documents → huge prefills. vLLM V1 chunks prefills by default (decodes are scheduled first), so the batch isn't frozen, but long prefills consume the max_num_batched_tokens budget for many steps and other prompts queue. Fix: gateway input-length cap; lower max_num_batched_tokens on the latency-sensitive pool (smaller → better ITL, larger → better TTFT/throughput per vLLM docs); route very long requests to a separate pool. (See episode 2.)

Handling interruptions and "why not X"

  • Interruption: answer in 2–3 sentences → "Coming back to the drawing, the question has reached retrieval." If it belongs to a later segment: "That's one of the decisions I'll cover in two minutes — can I come back to it?" — then do.
  • "Why not X?" three beats: (1) what X is genuinely good at, (2) the specific constraint that ruled it out for you, (3) what would change your mind. Never trash X; never "we just preferred it".
  • I vs we: "we" for the system, "I" for what you owned; say who owned the rest.

Role & expectations: questions to ask

  1. What does success look like in this role at 90 days and at one year?
  2. How does an agent/model change reach production — which evaluation gates and model-risk approvals, and how long does it take?
  3. Who owns evaluation sets / golden data — engineering, the business, or a risk function?
  4. Hosted vs self-hosted models: what's the split today and is it changing?
  5. What is on-call like for these systems, and what was the last serious incident?
  6. What does the team wish it had done differently on its current agent platform?

Key terms

Term Meaning
TTFT Time to first token — queueing + prefill (+ your retrieval if measured at the app)
ITL / TPOT Inter-token latency / time per output token — decode speed
E2E latency Request start to final token (plus post-processing such as citation checks)
Goodput Requests/s that meet all latency SLOs, not raw throughput
Chunked prefill Splitting long prompt prefill into slices interleaved with decode (on by default in vLLM V1, bounded by max_num_batched_tokens)
Trust boundary The infrastructure perimeter client data may not leave unredacted
Entitlement filtering Access control applied inside the retrieval query, not post-hoc
Citation checker Post-generation guard verifying each claim is supported by a cited retrieved section

5 interview questions with strong answers

Q1. Walk me through the architecture of a project you built. "Ten minutes: problem, constraints, architecture, decisions, numbers, failures, next — interrupt any time." Then problem in business terms with a number, constraints (residency, audit, entitlements, latency SLO, scale), and the architecture drawn by following one request left to right, stating what you personally owned.

Q2. Which latency metrics did you measure and why? TTFT (perceived responsiveness in streaming UI), inter-token latency (streaming smoothness), end-to-end including post-generation checks (when the user can act), and a per-component breakdown (where to optimize) — all as p95/p99, not averages — plus queue depth as the leading indicator and autoscaling signal. Mention the vLLM histogram names you scraped.

Q3. What frameworks did you use for LLM deployment, and why not the alternatives? vLLM on Kubernetes for PagedAttention, continuous batching, prefix caching and Prometheus metrics; considered SGLang (prefix reuse, structured output) and TensorRT-LLM (throughput, heavier builds); TGI is in maintenance mode since Dec 2025 so it would need a specific reason. Hosted model behind a gateway for redacted hard queries. Would switch engines only on a benchmark win on our own traffic.

Q4. Tell me about something that went wrong. Symptom → root cause → fix → guardrail. E.g. superseded section served with valid-looking citation because deletions left orphaned chunks; fixed with version-stamped section chunks, tombstones and nightly reconciliation.

Q5. Why not just use a managed hosted API? Acknowledge: faster to ship, stronger frontier models. Constraint: client data couldn't leave the boundary unredacted and redaction hurt quality on the questions that mattered. Would revisit with an in-region isolated deployment that passes vendor-risk review.

How does an agent or model change get to production here — what evaluation gates and model-risk approvals does it pass, and how long does that usually take? The problem: staff at a regulated financial firm needed fast, cited answers from tens of thousands of policy and product documents, where a wrong answer is a compliance risk. Constraints: client data couldn't leave our boundary, every answer had to be auditable to document versions, per-user entitlements, and first token within about one and a half seconds at p ninety-five. The architecture: a gateway doing auth, entitlements and P I I redaction; a Lang Graph orchestrator that routes between retrieve, answer, clarify, or refuse; version-aware hybrid retrieval with entitlement filters inside the search; v L L M on Kubernetes scaled on queue depth, with an approved hosted model for redacted hard queries; a citation checker; and an audit log under everything. I owned retrieval versioning and the citation checker — let me draw it as a question travels through." Traps to avoid: starting with the tech stack instead of the problem. Spending half your time on ingestion. Having no numbers, or averages instead of percentiles. Presenting a system with no failures — nobody believes it. Getting lost after an interruption. And ending the role conversation with "no questions." [PAUSE 2s] ## Sixty-second recap Back to the whiteboard. Across the top, the ribbon of ten minutes, seven segments, left to right.
