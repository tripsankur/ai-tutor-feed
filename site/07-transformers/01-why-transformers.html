From RNNs to Attention: Why Transformers Won

18 min listen · Why the transformer replaced recurrent networks: a single messenger walking a shelf of words versus a web of direct threads between every word — parallel training, short paths between distant words, and the quadratic bill that came with it.

Facts verified: 2026-09-25

One-sentence thesis

Transformers won not because of attention (which RNNs already had since 2014) but because they removed recurrence entirely — every token connects directly to every other token, so training parallelizes across the sequence and distant tokens are one hop apart; the price is compute and memory that grow with the square of sequence length.

The mental image: the shelf, the messenger, the web

  • Shelf of word tiles, left to right = the input sequence.
  • Messenger walking below the shelf with a fixed-size backpack = RNN with hidden state. Must walk step by step (no parallelism), backpack forgets the far-left tiles, lessons fade on the long walk back (vanishing gradients).
  • Snapshots under each tile that a second messenger can look back at = Bahdanau attention (2014) bolted onto RNNs.
  • Web of strings above the shelf, every tile to every tile, strings pulled tight or slack = self-attention (2017). All strings computed at once; one hop between any two tiles. Strings = n² → the quadratic bill.

Timeline

Year Milestone Why it mattered
1997 LSTM (Hochreiter & Schmidhuber) Gated "backpack" eased vanishing gradients; still sequential
2014 Seq2seq encoder-decoder RNNs Whole sentence squeezed into one fixed vector at the handoff
2014 Bahdanau, Cho, Bengio — additive attention Decoder looks back over all encoder states; big translation gains
2017 Vaswani et al. — "Attention Is All You Need" Recurrence removed; WMT14 En-De 28.4 BLEU; big model trained 3.5 days on 8 P100 GPUs
2022 FlashAttention Exact attention with linear memory via tiling; compute still quadratic
2025–26 Hybrid stacks (e.g. Qwen3-Next / Qwen3.5: 3 Gated DeltaNet layers per 1 full-attention layer; Nemotron-H: Mamba-2 + attention) Linear-time "smarter messengers" for most layers, full attention every few layers

Complexity comparison (from the 2017 paper, n = sequence length, d = width)

Layer type Compute per layer Sequential ops Max path length
Self-attention O(n²·d) O(1) O(1)
Recurrent O(n·d²) O(n) O(n)
Convolutional (kernel k) O(k·n·d²) O(1) O(log_k n)

Concrete: 1,000 tokens → 1M attention scores per head per layer; 100,000 tokens → 10B. Doubling context ≈ 4× attention work.

5 interview questions

Q1. Why did transformers replace RNNs? RNNs are sequential through a fixed-size hidden state: no parallelism along the sequence and O(n) path length between distant tokens (forgetting, vanishing gradients). Attention already existed on RNNs; the transformer removed recurrence, giving O(1) path length and turning the sequence into large parallel matmuls that GPUs are built for — which unlocked scale. Price: O(n²) attention and explicit position encoding.

Q2. What are the main weaknesses of transformers? Quadratic attention compute/memory in sequence length; hard context limits; permutation invariance (position must be added, and the choice limits length generalization); data hunger from weak inductive bias; at inference, a KV cache that grows linearly with context and dominates serving memory.

Q3. What did "Attention Is All You Need" actually change versus 2014 attention? 2014 attention was an add-on letting an RNN decoder look back at encoder states, but the encoder/decoder still walked sequentially. The 2017 paper dropped recurrence and convolution entirely and used self-attention inside both encoder and decoder, so all positions are processed simultaneously.

Q4. When would you not use a transformer? Small labeled datasets, short narrow sequences, tight latency/edge budgets — a small RNN/CNN or gradient-boosted model on features may be cheaper and as accurate. For extremely long streams, consider SSM or hybrid architectures.

Q5. Is attention being replaced? Partially. 2025–26 open-weight flagships increasingly use hybrids: mostly linear-time layers (Mamba-2, Gated DeltaNet) with full softmax attention every few layers (e.g. 3:1 in Qwen3-Next), which keeps exact long-range retrieval while making long-context throughput scale far more gently. Pure attention remains the default elsewhere.
